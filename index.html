Our Mission

Seeking God together through prayer and His Word.

🙏

A Life of Prayer

Our mission is to encourage believers to develop a consistent life of prayer. We believe prayer is not simply an activity reserved for difficult moments; it is a relationship with God through which we seek His presence, wisdom, direction and will.

📖

Standing on God's Word

We desire to build our prayer life on Scripture. Through Bible study, teaching and prayer, we encourage one another to understand God's promises and respond to His Word with faith, obedience and humility.

🤝

Praying for Others

We also carry one another in prayer. We pray for families, churches, communities, students, leaders and people who need encouragement. Our desire is to become a praying community that faithfully intercedes for others.

Our Vision

To become a community shaped by prayer.

Our Vision for the Future

We envision a generation that knows God personally and understands the importance of prayer. We want young people and believers to discover that prayer is not merely a religious routine but a meaningful conversation with God that shapes character, decisions and everyday life.


We also envision a united prayer community where people encourage one another to remain faithful. Through regular prayer meetings, Scripture-based teaching, prayer reminders and fellowship, we want to help create a culture in which seeking God becomes a natural and consistent part of life.


Our ultimate desire is to see the Prayer Team serve faithfully within Hayole Project Fellowship and contribute to its spiritual life through prayer, unity, service and biblical encouragement.

Bible-Based Teaching

Lessons for developing a deeper prayer life.

Lesson 1 — Pray With Faith

Biblical prayer is an expression of trust in God. When we pray, we acknowledge that God knows what we need and that His wisdom is greater than ours. Faith does not mean that we demand a particular answer from God. Instead, faith teaches us to bring our requests honestly before Him while trusting His character and wisdom.


Jesus taught His disciples to pray with confidence in God's care. Therefore, when we pray, we should not approach God only because we are afraid or desperate. We can approach Him because He is our Father and because He invites us to seek Him.

Lesson 2 — Pray According to God's Will

Prayer is not about making God follow our plans. Prayer also changes the person who prays. As we spend time with God and study Scripture, our desires can become increasingly aligned with His purposes.


Jesus demonstrated this attitude when He prayed in the garden, submitting His desire to the Father's will. A mature prayer life therefore includes both asking and surrendering. We can tell God what we desire while remaining willing to trust His answer.

Lesson 3 — Pray for Others

Scripture repeatedly presents prayer as something believers do for other people. Intercession teaches us to look beyond our own needs. We can pray for our families, friends, churches, communities, leaders and people who are experiencing difficulties.


When we pray for others, we become participants in a ministry of encouragement. We may not always know what another person is experiencing, but we can bring their needs before God and ask Him to work according to His wisdom.

People Who Prayed

Biblical examples that encourage us to seek God.

Daniel

Daniel remained committed to prayer even when his circumstances became dangerous. Scripture describes him continuing to pray regularly toward Jerusalem. His example teaches us that prayer should not disappear when pressure increases.


Daniel's prayer life was connected with his relationship with God. He did not pray only when he needed something. Prayer was already part of his daily life.


Daniel 6:10

Hannah

Hannah brought her deep sorrow before God and prayed honestly. Her story reminds us that prayer gives us a place to bring pain, hopes and personal struggles before the Lord.


Hannah's story also teaches us about surrender. After bringing her request to God, she continued forward trusting Him.


1 Samuel 1:10–18

Nehemiah

Nehemiah responded to the condition of Jerusalem with fasting and prayer. Before taking action, he sought God and confessed the sins of his people.


His example shows that prayer and action are not enemies. Prayer can prepare us to act wisely and courageously.


Nehemiah 1:4–11

Prayer Team & Hayole Project Fellowship

The Prayer Team is a dedicated prayer ministry serving within Hayole Project Fellowship. We exist to encourage believers to seek God together, pray for one another and support the spiritual life of the fellowship through faithful intercession.


As a team, we value unity, Scripture, humility, consistency and genuine love for people. Prayer is not separate from fellowship; it is one of the ways we serve one another and seek God's direction for the community.


Everyone who joins the Prayer Team is encouraged to participate faithfully, respect the agreed prayer schedule and help create an environment where people can grow in their relationship with God.
